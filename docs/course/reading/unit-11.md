## 核心内容

### 11.1 先把流程焊死：5 分钟/篇的节奏

前五课你学的是零件，本课把它们装成一台机器。每篇文章按这个节拍走：

| 时间 | 动作 | 用到的课 | 产出 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 第 1 分钟 | **略读定型**：读标题+首末段，判断文体 | 第 10 课 | 一句话：这是说明文/议论文/新闻/广告，考点在哪几段 |
| 第 2 分钟 | **看题干**：读 5 个题干圈定位词（数字、专有名词、核心名词），不看选项 | 第 4~6 课 | 一张定位路线图 |
| 第 3~4 分钟 | **定位 + 同义替换**：回原文找到定位句，比对选项和原文的改写关系 | 第 7~8 课 | 每题锁定两个候选 |
| 第 5 分钟 | **排除法收尾**：用极端词、范围伸缩、无据脑补三大检查器清掉错误项 | 第 7~9 课 | 最终答案 |

节奏纪律：卡在任何一题超过 60 秒，先跳过，做完再回头。5 道题的分值是均等的，你的时间也该如此。

### 11.2 Passage 1：四六级风格·说明文（科技生活，约 250 词）

> **One Number, Many Models: How Your Weather App Makes a Forecast**
>
> When you check your weather app, the answer arrives in seconds: a 70 percent chance of rain tomorrow. Behind that single number stands one of the largest computing operations on Earth.
>
> Every day, thousands of weather stations, balloons, ships, and satellites send in more than a million observations: temperature, pressure, wind, and humidity. Supercomputers feed these numbers into mathematical models of the atmosphere, which divide the sky into millions of imaginary boxes and calculate how each box will affect its neighbors over the coming hours.
>
> The models, however, are not perfect. The atmosphere is a noisy system in which a small change in one place can grow into a large difference somewhere else. To deal with this, modern forecasters run each model dozens of times, with slightly different starting conditions. If 70 percent of these runs produce rain, the app reports a 70 percent chance of rain. A probability, in other words, is not a promise; it is a vote among possible futures.
>
> The results have been remarkable. A five-day forecast today is roughly as accurate as a one-day forecast was in 1980, and sudden disasters like tornadoes are now spotted minutes earlier, minutes that save lives.
>
> So the next time you open your app and complain that it was wrong, remember what it beat to get there: the chaos of a planet-sized ocean of air, reduced, for your convenience, to a single honest number.

**第 1 分钟定型**：标题即话题（天气 App 如何做预报），首段无立场，第二、三段讲机制——说明文。地图：主旨看首段，细节盯机制段，态度默认 objective。

**Q1（主旨题）What is the passage mainly about?**
A. How weather apps turn millions of observations into a single forecast.
B. Why weather apps are wrong so often.
C. The history of weather satellites.
D. How to understand the rain probability in apps.

全流程：主旨题三步——①首段：一个 70% 的数字背后是巨型计算；②各段首句：观测→模型→概率的含义→准确度提升；③合成：A"天气 App 如何把观测变成预报"覆盖全文。B 与末段语气相反（作者是在替 App 说话）；C 只是第二段一个词；D 只是第三段一角。**答案 A**。

**Q2（细节题）Why do forecasters run each model dozens of times?**
A. Because supercomputers have spare power.
B. Because a small change in the atmosphere can grow into a large difference.
C. Because stations send too much data to process once.
D. To make the app look more scientific.

全流程：题干定位词 run each model dozens of times 锁定第三段。定位句：The atmosphere is a **noisy system** in which **a small change... can grow into a large difference**. To deal with this...——"To deal with this"的 this 就是原因。B 是近义改写。A、C 无据，D 脑补动机。**答案 B**。

**Q3（词义题）The word "spotted" (Para. 4) most likely means ___.**
A. detected and reported　B. photographed　C. repaired　D. prevented

全流程：定位句：tornadoes are now **spotted** minutes earlier。语境：灾难来临前更早被"发现"，才能多出救命的几分钟（minutes that save lives）。代入 A"探测并报告"，通；D"阻止"龙卷风——人类还没这本事，原文说的是预警不是阻止。**答案 A**。

**Q4（推理题）What can we infer about a "70 percent chance of rain" in the app?**
A. Rain appeared in about 70 percent of the model runs.
B. It rained on 70 percent of similar days in the past.
C. Rain will fall on 70 percent of the city's area.
D. Rain will last for 70 percent of the day.

全流程：回到概率的定义句（第三段末）：If 70 percent of these **runs** produce rain, the app reports 70 percent。一步推断：这个百分比来自模型运行结果的"投票"。B（历史统计）、C（面积）、D（时长）全是把概率安到错误的维度上——范围偷换。**答案 A**。

**Q5（态度题）The author's attitude toward modern weather forecasting is ___.**
A. objective　B. critical　C. ironic　D. worried

全流程：说明文找态度（[第 9 课](./unit-09.md)）：全文有没有 fortunately / unfortunately？有没有 but 后的重锤？有没有 claim 式阴阳？都没有。末段虽有 "honest number" 的赞赏，但整体是介绍机制+摆准确率数据，作者隐身。**答案 A**。教训：说明文别硬找态度，objective 就是证据充分的答案。

### 11.3 Passage 2：四六级风格·议论文（教育话题，约 250 词）

> **The Lost Art of the Pencil**
>
> When was the last time you wrote more than a shopping list by hand? For a growing number of students, the answer is "last handwriting class"—if their school still has one. As tablets replace notebooks, several countries are quietly reducing handwriting practice. This is a mistake, and the cost is higher than it looks.
>
> Defenders of keyboards point out, correctly, that children type faster and edit more easily on screens. True. But handwriting does things a keyboard cannot. Brain-imaging studies show that forming letters by hand activates regions tied to reading and memory far more strongly than pressing keys. Children who learn letters by writing them remember them better; adults who take notes by hand remember more of a lecture, because slow writing forces the brain to summarize instead of copying.
>
> There is also a quieter argument: handwriting is personal. A typed apology looks like every other typed apology; a handwritten one carries evidence of time and effort. Schools teach more than skills; they teach what we value.
>
> I do not argue for banning screens—typing is now as basic as riding a bike. But "either-or" is the wrong frame. Thirty minutes of daily handwriting, kept through primary school, costs little and protects much. If we let the pencil die quietly, we will not miss it until we discover what grew on it: attention, memory, and a small, human trace of who we are.

**第 1 分钟定型**：首段末句 This is a mistake——亮立场，议论文。地图：主旨=首段末句的立场；态度=末段；细节盯第二段论证。

**Q1（主旨题）What is the main purpose of the passage?**
A. To argue that handwriting still deserves a place in schools.
B. To prove that typing and screens are harmful to children.
C. To describe how handwriting developed through history.
D. To compare two brain-imaging studies.

全流程：议论文主旨=作者立场=首段末句（reducing handwriting practice is a mistake）+末段（每天 30 分钟，代价小保护多）。A 是立场的正面表述。B 超纲（作者明确说 typing is as basic as riding a bike，不反屏幕）；C 无历史内容；D 只是第二段一个证据。**答案 A**。

**Q2（细节题）According to brain-imaging studies, handwriting ___.**
A. activates brain regions linked to reading and memory more strongly than typing
B. is faster than typing for most children
C. improves eyesight along with memory
D. helps adults only when they summarize first

全流程：定位词 brain-imaging 锁定第二段。定位句：forming letters by hand **activates regions tied to reading and memory far more strongly than** pressing keys。A 是近义改写（tied to = linked to）。B 与让步段相反（打字更快，作者承认）；C 眼睛无据；D "only" 极端且偷换（是慢书写迫使总结，不是先总结才有效）。**答案 A**。

**Q3（推理题）What does the author imply about slow writing?**
A. Its slowness is exactly what helps the brain remember.
B. It prevents students from finishing lectures on time.
C. It was useful only before computers existed.
D. It makes copying notes more accurate.

全流程：定位句：adults who take notes by hand remember more of a lecture, **because slow writing forces the brain to summarize** instead of copying。一步推断：慢不是缺陷，是功能——慢逼你消化。A 就是这句的近义改写。B 曲解；C 无据；D 相反（恰恰是不逐字抄写）。**答案 A**。

**Q4（词义题）By saying "either-or" is the wrong frame, the author means that ___.**
A. handwriting and typing should not be treated as mutually exclusive choices
B. one must eventually pick a side in the debate
C. both handwriting and typing are worthless
D. either choice works equally well

全流程：定位末段：I do not argue for banning screens... **But "either-or" is the wrong frame.** Thirty minutes of daily handwriting...——作者两边都要：打字照学，写字保留。A"不应视为二选一"是原句改写。B 反着说；C、D 无据。**答案 A**。

**Q5（态度题）The author's attitude toward keeping handwriting in schools is ___.**
A. supportive　B. neutral　C. opposed　D. uncertain

全流程：态度证据链三处：①首段 This is a **mistake**（反对砍书法课）；②末段 costs little and **protects much**（保留的好处）；③末句 If we let the pencil **die quietly**, we will not **miss it until**...（警世收尾）。三处全正面，零处中立证据。**答案 A**。

### 11.4 Passage 3：考研/雅思风格·评论文（社会现象，约 300 词，含长难句拆解）

> **Seeing Is Believing, or Just Photographing?**
>
> At famous viewpoints from Kyoto to Barcelona, a new ritual has taken shape: visitors arrive, turn their backs on the scene, hold up their phones, take the same photograph, and leave. The whole performance often takes less time than the wait for it. Critics have named these travelers "tourist photographers"—people who collect pictures of places rather than experiences of them.
>
> The habit is easy to mock, but its causes deserve sympathy. Travel is expensive and brief; a photograph is proof that the money and the journey were real. Social media, meanwhile, has standardized beauty into a short list of approved angles, and human beings, being social animals, find comfort in doing what everyone else is doing. A tourist who walks past the famous frame may feel, vaguely, that the trip is incomplete.
>
> The costs, however, are no longer vague. Overcrowded spots have forced cities from Amsterdam to Dubrovnik to limit visitors and even to close certain streets. Psychologists add a subtler loss: people who photograph an experience continuously remember less of it, because attention spent on the camera is attention stolen from the moment.
>
> None of this means travelers must leave their phones at home. The reasonable goal is to reverse the order of the ritual: look first, for as long as the looking lasts, and let the photograph be a souvenir of attention rather than a substitute for it. Some museums now encourage exactly this, asking visitors to hold their cameras for the final five minutes of each room. It is a small rule, and perhaps a hopeful one. A place is not a box to be ticked; it is a room you were briefly allowed to enter, and the point of entering was always to see.

**第 1 分钟定型**：社会现象 + 让步（easy to mock, but）+ 末段给建议（look first）——评论文（议论的近亲）。地图：作者态度藏在末段；长难句在第二、三段。

**先拆两个长难句**（方法来自[拆句课](../parsing/index.md)：找谓语 → 剥插入语 → 切分句块）：

**长难句 ①**（第二段）：
> Social media, meanwhile, has standardized beauty into a short list of approved angles, and human beings, being social animals, find comfort in doing what everyone else is doing.

拆解三步：
1. 先找并列框架：and 连接两个完整句子，一刀切成两半。
2. 左半主干：Social media **has standardized** beauty into a list（社交媒体把美标准化成一份清单）；meanwhile 是连接性副词，剥掉。
3. 右半主干：human beings **find** comfort in doing...（人们在做事中找到安慰）；being social animals 是插入语（"作为群居动物"），先剥掉再读；what everyone else is doing 是 doing 的宾语从句（别人都在做的事）。
整句人话：社交媒体把"美"定成了少数几个官方角度，而人作为群居动物，跟风打卡才有安全感。

**长难句 ②**（第三段）：
> Psychologists add a subtler loss: people who photograph an experience continuously remember less of it, because attention spent on the camera is attention stolen from the moment.

拆解三步：
1. 冒号一刀：冒号前是主干（心理学家指出一种更微妙的损失），冒号后解释 loss 是什么。
2. 冒号后主句：people **remember** less of it；who photograph an experience continuously 是定语从句，挂在 people 身上（不停拍照的人）。
3. because 从句内部是一组平行结构：attention **spent on** the camera（花在相机上的注意力）is attention **stolen from** the moment（从当下偷走的注意力）——两个过去分词短语都是后置定语。
整句人话：一直拍照的人记住得更少，因为花在相机上的注意力，就是从当下偷走的注意力。

**Q1（主旨题）What is the author's overall view on photographing at tourist spots?**
A. It should be banned at all famous viewpoints.
B. Its causes are understandable, but it carries real costs; look first, photograph second.
C. It is a harmless habit that critics take too seriously.
D. It is caused entirely by the standardizing power of social media.

全流程：评论文主旨=让步+立场。让步（causes deserve sympathy）与立场（costs are no longer vague + look first）合成 B。A 极端（作者明说不必把手机留在家里）；C 无视第三段的代价；D 里 entirely 极端词，且原因不止社交媒体。**答案 B**。

**Q2（细节题）Why have some cities limited visitors?**
A. Because famous spots have become overcrowded.
B. Because visitors damage ancient cameras... 改：Because photographs mislead tourists about the places.
C. Because tourists steal items from museums.
D. Because hotels cannot hold the growing numbers.

全流程：定位词 cities... limit visitors 锁定第三段首句：**Overcrowded spots** have forced cities... to limit visitors。A 是 overcrowded 的近义改写。B、C、D 全是脑补。**答案 A**。

**Q3（推理题）What can we infer about people who photograph an experience continuously?**
A. Their memory of the experience becomes weaker.
B. They enjoy the moment more than others.
C. They take fewer photos than tourist photographers.
D. Their attention improves with practice.

全流程：长难句 ② 刚拆完：remember **less** of it。一步推断：记得更少 = 记忆更弱。A 是近义改写。B 相反；C 概念偷换（这是同一批人）；D 无据。**答案 A**。——看见没有，拆过句子再做推理题，等于开着地图走路。

**Q4（词义/指代题）In "a souvenir of attention rather than a substitute for it", "it" refers to ___.**
A. the attention　B. the camera　C. the photograph　D. the museum

全流程：指代题三步（[第 8 课](./unit-08.md)）：往前找最近单数名词——**attention**；单复数一致；代入验证：照片应是"注意力的纪念品"，而不是"注意力的替代品"（代替你亲眼看）。句义通顺且与全文主张（look first）咬合。**答案 A**。

**Q5（态度题）In the last paragraph, the author's tone toward the museums' new rule is ___.**
A. approving　B. ironic　C. doubtful　D. uninterested

全流程：末段证据：It is a small rule, and **perhaps a hopeful one**（虽小，但或许充满希望）——hopeful 直接报立场。后文金句（the point of entering was always to see）是抒情式赞同。无讽刺、无怀疑、无冷漠信号。**答案 A**。

### 11.5 错题归因：四类病因，四种药

做完题不是终点，归因才是。[语法课第 12 课](../grammar/unit-12.md)的归因理念同样适用于阅读：**错题不可怕，不知道为什么错才可怕。**每道错题四选一：

| 病因 | 典型症状 | 对策 | 对应课 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 定位错 | 答案解析一看就懂，但当时找错了段落/句子 | 练"题干圈词→原文扫词"的映射，题干名词必须原样或同义出现在定位句 | 第 4~6 课 |
| 替换没认出 | 定位句找对了，却没认出选项是它的改写 | 抄下"原句 vs 正确选项"的对照组，积累同义替换对 | 第 7~8 课 |
| 过度推断 | 选了那个"我觉得很合理"但原文没说的选项 | 用一步/二步推断标尺重新量一遍，警告自己常识不投票 | 第 7 课 |
| 词汇不认识 | 定位句里关键的词/短语看不懂 | 记入生词本，优先背"影响选项理解的词"，用拆句课+词根课拆长难词 | 第 8 课 + [词根课](../roots/index.md) |

归因纪律：每道错题在笔记里写三行——错误选项是什么、定位句是哪句、病因四选一。攒 20 道题，你的弱点分布会自己浮出水面。

## 常见误区

| ❌ 错误做法 | ✅ 正确做法 | 为什么 |
|---|---|---|
| 一篇文章从头精读到底，再慢悠悠看题 | 1 分钟略读定型，带著问题回原文定位 | 考试给的是 5 分钟不是 15 分钟；略读损失的是细节记忆，换来的是精准打击 |
| 题干和选项一起读，被错误选项带节奏 | 先只读题干圈定位词，选项等定位完再看 | 错误选项是精心设计的"先入为主"毒饵，先读原文再读选项，主动权在你手里 |
| 卡在一道题上死磕，后面全没时间 | 60 秒没头绪就跳过，标记后回头收拾 | 阅读考试是抢分游戏不是解谜游戏；一道题的价值不值得三道题陪葬 |
| 对完答案把错题抄一遍就完事 | 每道错题归因四选一，写"错误选项 vs 定位句"对照 | 抄题是体力劳动，归因才是认知升级；同一类病因反复出现，才知道该补哪一课 |

## 实战练习

**Passage A：说明文（约 215 词，先 30 秒定型，限时 5 分钟）**

> **Jet Lag: A Clock in Every Cell**
>
> Jump a few time zones and your body files a complaint: you feel sleepy at noon and wide awake at midnight. Travelers call it jet lag; scientists call it circadian misalignment—a fancy name for a simple disagreement.
>
> Your body runs on an internal clock, a cluster of cells in the brain that times sleep, hunger, and body temperature to a 24-hour cycle. This clock does not read a watch. It takes its cue mainly from light, which signals the brain that it is daytime and resets the cycle.
>
> The problem with flying is speed. The clock can adjust, but only by about one hour per day, because it evolved for travelers who walked. When you cross six time zones in six hours, you arrive faster than your biology can follow: your watch says afternoon, but your cells are still somewhere over the ocean.
>
> Light remains the fastest fix, used in the right direction at the right time—morning light after flying east, evening light after flying west. Some travelers also adjust their sleep an hour earlier for a few nights before departure, buying their cells a head start. What no one can buy is instant reset; the clock that took millions of years to build refuses to be rushed by a boarding pass.

**1. The passage mainly explains ___.**

- A. why jet lag happens and how the body clock can be helped to adjust
- B. which airlines offer the best sleep schedules
- C. how scientists discovered the brain's timing cells
- D. why some people never suffer from jet lag

**2. The internal clock takes its cue mainly from ___.**

- A. watch time
- B. light
- C. hunger
- D. body temperature

**3. The phrase "takes its cue from" (Para. 2) most likely means ___.**

- A. gets its timing signal from
- B. pays no attention to
- C. measures exactly
- D. suffers damage from

**4. What can we infer about crossing six time zones by plane?**

- A. The body may need about six days to fully adjust.
- B. Most travelers adjust within one day.
- C. Six time zones is the limit the clock can handle.
- D. The direction of travel does not matter.

**5. Adjusting sleep earlier for a few nights before departure works because it ___.**

- A. gives the body clock a head start
- B. removes all light exposure
- C. shortens the flight itself
- D. resets the clock instantly

**Passage B：议论文（约 230 词，先 30 秒定型，限时 5 分钟）**

> **Locking Phones, Opening Classrooms**
>
> Last term, our school tried something bold: every student placed their phone in a locked pouch at the morning bell and got it back at dismissal. The complaints lasted about a week. The changes lasted longer.
>
> Teachers reported the first difference within days: students looked up. Conversations in the corridors grew louder, and, to everyone's surprise, the lunch queue became a place for jokes instead of scrolling. Attendance in afternoon classes improved slightly, and several students who had been silent for months began to raise their hands.
>
> Critics of phone bans argue that students must learn self-control, not have it imposed. I agree in principle—and I have seen the principle fail in practice. Asking a teenager to study beside a phone is like asking a dieter to work in a bakery: possible, but needlessly cruel. Willpower is a muscle; it trains best when the temptation is at a distance first and near later.
>
> None of this means schools should pretend phones do not exist. Digital literacy matters, and it can be taught in computer class, where the phones stay in the pouch until needed. It is a schedule with clear office hours.

**Passage A：倒时差（说明文）**

定型自检：话题（倒时差的原理），无立场，讲机制——说明文。主旨看首段，细节盯第二、三段。

1. **A**。主旨题。首段抛现象（时差是什么），二段讲机制（生物钟靠光线校准），三段讲矛盾（飞机太快、钟太慢），四段给对策（方向性用光+提前调睡眠）。A 覆盖全部。B 航空公司无据；C 发现史无据；D "有人从不倒时差"无据。
2. **B**。细节题。定位第二段：It **takes its cue mainly from light**。同义改写即答案。A 有原话反证（This clock does not read a watch）；C、D 是钟"管理"的对象，不是线索来源。
3. **A**。词义题。定位句：This clock does not read a watch. It **takes its cue mainly from** light, which... **resets** the cycle。上下文：不读表 → 靠 ___ 光 → 校准周期。代入 A"从……获得定时信号"，通。B 与 resets 矛盾；C、D 方向不符。
4. **A**。推理题。定位句：The clock can adjust, but only by **about one hour per day**。一步推断：6 个时区 × 每天调 1 小时 ≈ 6 天。B 与此矛盾；C 极端；D 相反（第四段明确方向很重要：东飞早晨光、西飞傍晚光）。
5. **A**。细节题。定位句：adjusting their sleep an hour earlier..., **buying their cells a head start**。A"给生物钟一个提前量"是近义改写。B、C、D 无据或极端（instant 与末句 refuses to be rushed 相反）。

**Passage B：手机收纳袋（议论文）**

定型自检：作者亲历 + 让步反驳 + 末段立场——议论文。态度盯末段，细节盯一、二段。

6. **A**。态度题。证据链：①标题 Locking Phones, **Opening** Classrooms（押头韵的正面并列）；②The complaints lasted about a week. **The changes lasted longer.**（对比修辞站队）；③末段 A locked pouch is **not a war** on technology. It is **a schedule** with clear office hours.（定性辩护）。三处正面。B/C/D 与证据不符。
7. **A**。细节题。定位第二段：**Attendance in afternoon classes improved slightly**。B 极端（complaints lasted about a week ≠ 立刻消失）；C 无据；D 相反（走廊更热闹，不是更安静）。
8. **A**。词义题。定位句：students must learn self-control, not have it **imposed**。对比结构：自己学 vs 被强行 ___。代入 A"强加"，与 critics 的抱怨逻辑（反对强制）咬合。B、C、D 与"批评者反对"的方向不符。
9. **A**。推理题。定位句：possible, but **needlessly cruel** + 下一句：Willpower... trains best when the temptation is **at a distance** first。一步推断：先把诱惑拿远，比贴脸硬扛更有效——即 A"移除诱惑比近距离抵抗更容易"。B 就事论事跑偏；C 极端（almost always）；D 幽默选项，无据。
10. **A**。推理/主旨题。末段：Digital literacy matters... taught in computer class（手机该学，但限时）；The question is... **where the phones live** during the seven hours（问题不是要不要，是什么时候）。合成 A"手机在校内有恰当的时间与位置"。B 极端（不是永禁）；C 极端；D 脑补单一原因。
